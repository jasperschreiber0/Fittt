Build a production-quality MVP of a mobile-first web app for a 90-day health challenge between small groups of friends.

## PRODUCT THESIS

This is NOT another MyFitnessPal, calorie counter, macro tracker, workout logger or generic habit tracker.

The core idea is:

**Fitness for people who have a life.**

The target user generally wants to eat well, train regularly and improve their body composition, but they also have dinners, weddings, parties, holidays, beers with friends and other social events.

Traditional fitness apps create too much friction and often treat any deviation as failure.

This product should instead answer:

**“Am I still on track?”**

The experience should require as little effort as possible.

The primary interaction is talking naturally to the app.

Example:

> “Pretty good today. Eggs and hash browns for breakfast, chicken pesto pasta for lunch, protein shake, steak and veggies for dinner. Did weights and around 8,000 steps.”

The system should extract and estimate:

- foods
- quantities where possible
- estimated calorie range
- estimated protein
- alcohol
- exercise
- steps
- confidence
- assumptions

The user should normally only need to tap:

**Looks right ✓**

Do not create an experience requiring users to search food databases, scan barcodes, manually enter macros or complete long forms.

---

# CORE PRODUCT PRINCIPLES

1. Voice first.
2. Natural language first.
3. Estimates rather than fake precision.
4. Weekly trajectory matters more than individual days.
5. Planned social events are part of the program.
6. Recovery matters more than punishment.
7. Behaviour consistency matters more than calorie perfection.
8. Private body metrics; shared accountability.
9. Daily interaction should normally take under 10 seconds.
10. Every feature must justify the friction it creates.

If a feature adds complexity without materially helping adherence or understanding, do not build it.

# USER EXPERIENCE

Design primarily for iPhone-sized screens but make it responsive.

Make it installable as a PWA.

The app should feel extremely simple, polished and premium.

Avoid a clinical diet-app appearance.

Navigation should contain no more than:

**Today | Progress | Friends | Events | Settings**

Today is always the default screen.

# ONBOARDING

Keep onboarding extremely short.

Collect:

- first name
- age
- sex
- height
- current weight
- optional waist measurement
- goal: lose fat / maintain / gain
- optional target weight
- typical weekly training frequency
- approximate daily activity/steps
- approximate current alcohol frequency
- desired challenge difficulty
- challenge start date

Calculate an estimated baseline:

- BMR
- TDEE
- maintenance calorie range
- suggested calorie target/range
- suggested protein target
- approximate weekly energy target

Clearly label estimates as estimates.

Allow targets to be edited.

Do NOT force calorie counting.

Users should be able to complete onboarding quickly and refine their profile later.

---

# CHALLENGES

A user can:

- create a 90-day challenge
- invite friends using a link/code
- join an existing challenge

Initially optimise for groups of 2–10 rather than large social networks.

Each participant has their own personalised targets.

Challenge scoring is standardised so friends with different goals can compete fairly.

Do NOT rank people by:

- lowest calories
- most weight lost
- lowest body weight
- eating the least

Competition should reward adherence and consistency.

# VOICE-FIRST LOGGING

This is the most important feature.

The Today screen should prominently feature:

**🎙 Log my day**

Use browser/device speech-to-text where appropriate, with typed natural-language input as fallback.

Users can say things such as:

> “Good day. Eggs and toast breakfast, chicken wrap lunch, protein shake, steak and veggies dinner. Gym this morning. No alcohol.”

Or:

> “Big one. Probably 12 schooners, half a bottle of wine, two slices of pizza and a kebab.”

Or:

> “Add three beers and a burger.”

Or:

> “Dinner was chicken curry and rice. That's me done.”

The AI must convert this into structured data.

Create a structured schema such as:

{
  foods: [],
  drinks: [],
  estimated_calories_low,
  estimated_calories_high,
  estimated_protein_low,
  estimated_protein_high,
  alcohol_standard_drinks_estimate,
  exercise: [],
  steps,
  assumptions: [],
  confidence,
  clarification_required
}

Do not pretend nutrition estimates are exact.

Display ranges where appropriate.

Example:

**Estimated intake: 2,400–2,700 kcal**

not:

**2,563 kcal**

If ambiguity materially changes the estimate, ask ONE concise clarification.

Example:

> “When you said two pizzas, did you mean two slices or two whole pizzas?”

If the uncertainty is relatively insignificant, make a reasonable assumption and show it.

The goal is minimum friction, not perfect dietary measurement.

---

# CONVERSATIONAL DAILY LOG

Allow multiple entries throughout the day.

Example:

Morning:
> “Two eggs, two hash browns and coffee.”

Afternoon:
> “Add a chicken pesto wrap and protein shake.”

Evening:
> “Steak and vegetables for dinner. Gym done. That's me.”

The system should intelligently update the day's existing record rather than creating three separate days.

Users must be able to correct AI interpretation easily.

Provide:

**Looks right ✓**

and

**Adjust**

Do not force users through every extracted food item unless they choose Adjust.

---

# FAST MODE

Voice cannot be mandatory.

For ordinary days provide an ultra-fast alternative:

Food:
**On plan / Off plan**

Training:
**Done / Rest / Missed**

Alcohol:
**None / Planned / Unplanned**

Optional weight.

Save.

This should take approximately 5–10 seconds.

---

# GREEN DAYS

Create a simple daily status.

### GREEN

Normal day where the user broadly followed their plan.

### GOLD

Pre-planned social event.

### OFF PLAN

A day substantially outside the intended plan without being a planned event.

Avoid aggressive red failure screens.

One imperfect day should not psychologically destroy the challenge.

---

# GOLD EVENTS

This is a major differentiator.

Users can schedule a social event beforehand.

Examples:

- birthday
- wedding
- Christmas party
- dinner
- pub
- holiday
- work function

Tap:

**🎉 Add Gold Event**

Keep creation extremely simple:

Event name
Date
Optional event size:

**Dinner | Drinks | Big one**

A Gold Event is intentional flexibility, not a cheat day.

The app should never encourage starving before a social event.

Before the event, it can show useful context such as:

> “You've had a strong week and are currently around 1,600 kcal below your weekly target. Enjoy tonight and log it afterwards.”

Afterwards:

> “How did we go?”

The user should be able to speak the answer.

Example:

> “About 10 beers, burger and chips and probably four slices of pizza.”

Estimate the impact and allow confirmation.

---

# WEEKLY ENERGY BANK

Make this one of the main product concepts.

Instead of obsessing over daily calories, calculate estimated weekly energy balance.

Example:

**THIS WEEK**

Target: ~18,900 kcal

Estimated intake:
~17,200–18,000

Estimated position:
**~900–1,700 kcal under target**

Saturday:
**Gold Event 🎉**

Use ranges where uncertainty exists.

Answer the user's real question:

**“Can I enjoy Saturday and still be roughly on track?”**

Do not present calorie banking as permission or encouragement to restrict excessively before social events.

---

# “WHERE AM I AT?” AI

Add a conversational action:

**Ask**

Examples:

> “I've got dinner and drinks tonight. Where am I sitting?”

> “If I have six beers and a burger tonight what does that do to my week?”

> “I've got a wedding Saturday. Should I change anything this week?”

The AI should use:

- personalised targets
- current week's estimated intake
- training
- previous social events
- upcoming Gold Events
- weight trend if available

Then provide a short practical answer.

Do not provide extreme restriction or punishment recommendations.

---

# SUNDAY RESET

Build a weekly review.

This should be psychologically supportive without being cheesy.

Example:

**WEEK 4**

Adherence: 89%

Training: 5/5 ✓
Nutrition: 6/7 ✓
Alcohol-free days: 6
Gold Events: 1
Unplanned drinking: 0

Estimated weekly energy balance:
~500–1,000 kcal deficit

Weight trend:
−0.3 kg

Then:

**Next week's focus**

One or two useful recommendations.

If Saturday was excessive, do NOT recommend starvation, fasting or punishment exercise.

Instead:

- return to normal eating
- hydrate
- hit protein target
- normal movement
- resume training schedule

Core philosophy:

**Never miss twice.**

- weight

- optional progress photos
- estimated energy balance
- training adherence
- alcohol consumption
- Green/Gold days
- streak
- overall adherence

Weight should preferably be displayed using a rolling trend rather than making users react to daily fluctuations.

Progress photos must be private.

- first name

- overall adherence %

- Green days

Allow privacy settings.

Example leaderboard:

2. Sam — 88%
3. Tom — 84%

# GROUP MOMENTS

Add lightweight group achievements without creating a full social network.

Examples:

**Perfect Week**
Everyone finishes ≥85%.

**Dry Week**
Everyone completes the agreed alcohol-free period.

**Training Sweep**
Everyone reaches their personal training target.

**30 Green Days**
Group milestone.

Avoid feeds, DMs and complicated social functionality in V1.

# ALCOHOL

Alcohol is a first-class component of the product.

Track:

- alcohol-free days
- planned drinking events
- unplanned drinking events
- approximate standard drinks
- estimated alcohol calories

Allow natural Australian language where possible:

- schooner
- pint
- middy
- stubby
- bottle of wine
- spirits
- cocktails

The AI should understand that serving sizes vary and estimate accordingly.

Do not moralise about alcohol.

Focus on intentionality and trajectory.

---

# FRICTION REDUCTION

Continuously optimise for fewer taps.

Important:

- persistent login
- social login / magic link
- sensible defaults
- remember previous choices
- no unnecessary confirmation dialogs
- no mandatory food database
- no barcode scanning requirement
- no mandatory calorie entry
- no mandatory weighing
- no complicated workout logging
- no long daily questionnaires
- voice and natural language everywhere useful

Where possible, the user should be able to say:

> “Same breakfast as yesterday.”

or:

> “Normal weekday, gym done, no drinks.”

and have the system understand.

Support reusable personal food memory.

If a user frequently says:

> “my usual breakfast”

allow the system to learn what that refers to after confirmation.

This personal food memory is potentially valuable because estimates should improve over time without increasing friction.

---

# UNCERTAINTY ENGINE

Treat uncertainty as a product feature.

Every AI nutrition estimate should internally maintain:

- estimate
- range
- confidence
- assumptions

Over time learn frequently consumed meals and serving sizes.

Example:

First time:
“Chicken pesto pasta”
Confidence: low-medium

After the user has confirmed the same meal several times:
“My chicken pesto pasta”
Confidence: high

The product should therefore become more personalised and useful the longer someone uses it.

---

# TRAJECTORY ENGINE

This is another key differentiator.

Don't merely report what happened.

Continuously estimate:

**Given the user's current behaviour, where are they likely to finish Day 90?**

Example:

**DAY 31 / 90**

Current weight trend: 89.8 kg

Current trajectory:
**~87.1–88.0 kg by Day 90**

Target:
87.5 kg

**You're on track.**

Or:

**Current trajectory: ~89 kg**

Target: 87 kg

**Slightly behind target.**

Then suggest the smallest sustainable adjustment required.

Never respond with drastic calorie reductions.

This gives the app forward-looking value instead of being merely a diary.

---

# MINIMUM VIABLE DAY

Allow users to establish a minimum standard for chaotic days.

Example:

- protein target broadly met
- some fruit/vegetables
- no unplanned alcohol
- 30-minute walk

If the user cannot execute the full plan, the app can suggest:

**Save the day**

and show the minimum actions that keep momentum.

This should not become another complicated score.

---

# SCORING

Develop a transparent scoring model.

Prioritise:

- nutrition adherence
- training adherence
- alcohol intentionality
- consistency
- recovery after off-plan days

Do NOT reward eating dramatically below calorie targets.

Do NOT give additional points for excessive exercise.

Gold Events should not automatically destroy adherence.

An example weighting to prototype:

Nutrition adherence: 35%
Training adherence: 25%
Alcohol intentionality: 25%
Consistency/recovery: 15%

Test this and adjust if necessary.

Aim for:

**85%+ = successful challenge**

Make scoring explainable.

Users should always be able to tap their score and understand why it changed.

---

# NOTIFICATIONS

Keep notifications minimal.

Potential notification:

> “Haven't checked in today — 10 seconds and you're done.”

Do not nag repeatedly.

Optional Gold Event reminders can be useful.

Weekly review notification should be opt-in.

---

# AI ARCHITECTURE

Use AI only where it creates meaningful value.

Good AI use cases:

- natural-language parsing
- voice transcript interpretation
- food/drink estimation
- clarification questions
- personal food memory
- weekly review
- trajectory explanation
- event planning
- conversational “where am I at?” questions

Do NOT use an LLM for deterministic calculations.

Use application code for:

- BMR
- TDEE
- calorie arithmetic
- rolling averages
- adherence scores
- weekly balances
- trends
- leaderboard rankings

Require structured AI outputs validated against schemas.

Never trust free-form model output for calculations that can be deterministic.

# COMPETITIVE POSITIONING

Be aware that users can already track food, workouts and weight using products such as MyFitnessPal, MacroFactor, Lose It!, Strava, Whoop and general AI assistants.

Do not attempt to beat established products at their strongest feature.

Our wedge is:

**Low-friction, voice-first accountability for people balancing fitness with a real social life.**

Differentiators:

1. Speak instead of manually logging.
2. Estimates instead of fake precision.
3. Planned social events.
4. Weekly energy context.
5. Intentional vs unplanned alcohol.
6. Recovery after big days.
7. Small private friend challenges.
8. Personal food memory that reduces friction over time.
9. Forward-looking Day-90 trajectory.
10. “Am I still on track?” rather than “Did I perfectly hit today's macros?”

Protect that simplicity.

# SAFETY / PRODUCT BEHAVIOUR

Do not encourage:

- starvation
- purging
- punishment exercise
- extreme fasting to compensate for social events
- dangerous calorie restriction
- competitive weight loss between friends

If behaviour appears extreme, avoid optimising it further and encourage a more appropriate sustainable approach.

The product should reinforce consistency rather than guilt.
